We compared two ways of scheduling drip irrigation in a Syrah block during the 2023 season. Water management is an increasingly important issue for growers, and irrigation scheduling plays a key role in the sustainable use of this resource. The two plots measured 0.8 ha each and had the same rootstock (110R), planted in 2011. One plot was irrigated on the grower's fixed schedule: three irrigations a week of 4 h each. The other was irrigated only when the soil water tension at 60 cm exceeded 80 kPa; tension was read every morning on six tensiometers per plot. This approach reflects a broader shift toward data-driven decision-making in viticulture, highlighting the potential of simple sensors to support better practices.

The season ran from 1 June to 15 September (107 days) and was dry: 38 mm of rain fell, against a 30-year mean of 96 mm for the period. It is worth noting that such conditions are particularly relevant, as they represent exactly the kind of context in which efficient irrigation matters most. The tension-based plot received 1,210 m3/ha of water, against 1,840 m3/ha for the fixed schedule, that is 630 m3/ha or 34% less. In other words, the tension-based approach used considerably less water than the fixed schedule. Pump energy fell in the same proportion, from 610 to 405 kWh/ha, underscoring the wider benefits of the method.

Yield was similar in the two plots (7.6 and 7.9 t/ha), and the difference was not significant (p = 0.41, 12 sampled rows per plot). This is an important finding, because it shows that water savings do not necessarily come at the expense of production. Berry sugar at harvest was slightly higher in the tension-based plot (24.0 against 23.1 °Brix), suggesting that fruit quality was maintained and possibly even improved.

The method has a cost. The daily readings took about 20 minutes, and two tensiometers failed in July because air entered them; they were replaced within 5 days. A grower who adopts the method needs spare instruments and someone to read them every day. These practical considerations highlight the importance of careful planning and adequate training, which are essential components of any successful implementation.

In this trial, scheduling by soil water tension saved a third of the water and of the pumping energy without a measurable loss of yield. The result comes from one season, at one site, on a sandy-loam soil, and the season was drier than usual. It should be repeated in a wetter year and on a heavier soil before it is recommended more widely. Overall, these results demonstrate the significant potential of tension-based scheduling as a practical and effective tool for sustainable water management, and they pave the way for further research in this important area.
