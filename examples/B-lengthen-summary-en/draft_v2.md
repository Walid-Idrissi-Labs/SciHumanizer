We compared two ways of scheduling drip irrigation in a Syrah block during the 2023 season. The two plots measured 0.8 ha each and had the same rootstock (110R), planted in 2011. One plot was irrigated on the grower's fixed schedule: three irrigations a week of 4 h each. The other was irrigated only when the soil water tension at 60 cm exceeded 80 kPa; tension was read every morning on six tensiometers per plot.

The season ran from 1 June to 15 September (107 days) and was dry: 38 mm of rain fell, against a 30-year mean of 96 mm for the period. The tension-based plot received 1,210 m3/ha of water, against 1,840 m3/ha for the fixed schedule, that is 630 m3/ha or 34% less. Pump energy fell in the same proportion, from 610 to 405 kWh/ha.

Yield was similar in the two plots (7.6 and 7.9 t/ha), and the difference was not significant (p = 0.41, 12 sampled rows per plot). Berry sugar at harvest was slightly higher in the tension-based plot (24.0 against 23.1 °Brix).

The method has a cost. The daily readings took about 20 minutes, and two tensiometers failed in July because air entered them; they were replaced within 5 days. A grower who adopts the method needs spare instruments and someone to read them every day.

In this trial, scheduling by soil water tension saved a third of the water and of the pumping energy without a measurable loss of yield. The result comes from one season, at one site, on a sandy-loam soil, and the season was drier than usual. It should be repeated in a wetter year and on a heavier soil before it is recommended more widely.
