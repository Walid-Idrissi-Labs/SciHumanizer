Background: Heat-related morbidity is rising, but the contribution of intra-urban temperature differences is unclear. Methods: We linked daily land-surface temperature estimates at 1 km resolution to 1.9 million emergency admissions of adults aged 65+ in 12 European cities (2008-2018) and fitted distributed-lag non-linear models. Results: Admissions rose 11% (95% CI 8-14%) on days above the 95th temperature percentile compared with the median. The excess risk was 1.6 times higher in the hottest urban quintile than in the coolest. Respiratory and renal admissions drove most of the excess; cardiovascular admissions showed no clear association. Conclusions: Intra-urban heat exposure, not only city-wide temperature, shapes heat-related morbidity in older adults.
